Proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational (by contradiction) : 
- Assume $\sqrt{2}$ is rational so $\sqrt{2} = \frac{a}{b} : a,b \in \mathbb{Z}, b \neq 0$ 
- $\sqrt{2} = \frac{a}{b}$ so $2 = \frac{a^2}{b^2}$ 
- $a^2$ & $b^2$ will both have an even number of primes as they have been squared 
- Rearranging $2 = \frac{a^2}{b^2}$ gives $2 b^2 = a^2$ 
- This equation suggests that $a^2$ has an odd number of primes 
- This contradicts the assumption that $\sqrt{2}$ can be written as $\frac{a}{b}$ 
- Therefore $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational 

Proof that there are an infinite number of primes : 
- Assume There are a finite number of primes : $p_1,\; p_2,\; ...\; p_n$ 
- Create a new number $n = (p_1 \times p_2 \times\; ... \times p_n) + 1$ 
- $n$ is larger than any of the primes as it is their product plus 1 
- $n$ is not divisible by any of the primes as there will always be a remainder of 1 
- So either $n$ is a prime not in the original list or $n$ is divisible by a prime not in the original list 
- Therefore the original assumption was incorrect and there are infinite prime numbers
