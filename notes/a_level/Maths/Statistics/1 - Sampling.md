#Mathematics #Statistics 

Population is the entire group of items relevant to a specific investigation 
A sample is a set of items chosen from the population 
Sample statistics are an estimate for population statistics 
Sampling without replacement can help avoid duplicates but is not needed for infinite populations 

A census is an investigation of the entire population 
- Most accurate 
- Time consuming & expensive 
- Not applicable if testing to destruction 

## Random Sampling Methods 
- Simple random sample 
- Stratified sample 
- Systematic sample 
- Cluster sample 

Simple random sample : 
1) Each item in the sampling frame is given an id number 
2) Use a method of selecting $n$ random numbers in the range of the id numbers 
3) Those $n$ random numbers correspond to the items to be sampled 
- Easy, quick & cheap 
- No bias 
- Sampling frame needed 

Systematic sampling : 
1) Create a numbered sampling frame for the population 
2) Let $k=\frac{\text{population size}}{\text{sample size}}$ 
3) Generate a random number between 1 and $k$ and use this as the starting point 
4) Select every $k^{th}$ item in the sampling frame from this starting point 
- Easy, quick & cheap 
- Sampling frame needed 
- Can create bias if sampling frame not random (e.g. ordered by salary) 

Cluster sampling : 
1) Divide population into smaller sub-groups 
2) Use simple random sample to select sub-groups which will make up the sample 
3) If two-stage cluster sample then perform simple random sample on each cluster 
- Can reduce travel costs (e.g. for geographically large investigations) 
- Can be unrepresentative if clusters are not representative of entire population 

Stratified sampling : 
1) Split up population into representative groups called strata 
2) Work out how many items to sample from each strata : $\frac{\text{number in strata}}{\text{number in population}}\times\text{total sample size}$ 
3) Number items in each strata then do a simple random sample for each 
- Reflects population groups / structure 
- Guarantees proportional representation of strata 
- Population must be clearly classified 
- Sampling frame needed 

## Non-Random Sampling Methods 
- Opportunity sample 
- Quota sample 
- Self-selecting sample 

Opportunity sampling : 
1) Try to asses every item that meets a given criteria until the sample size is fulfilled 
- Easy, quick & cheap 
- Unlikely to provide representative sample especially if population >> sample size 
- Dependant on the conductor's biases 
- Non-responses not recorded (e.g. people who don't want to take the survey) 

Quota sampling : 
1) Work out size of each quota so proportions of each strata match population 
2) Pick sampling units until each quota is full 
3) Ignore items from filled quotas 
- Easy, quick & cheap 
- Allows smaller samples to be representative 
- No sampling frame required 
- Can introduce bias 
- Strata may be inaccurate 

Self-selecting sample : 
1) Ask people that meet a criteria to be part of your sample until sample size reached 
- Easy, quick & cheap 
- Specific type of people who may have stronger views / opinions 
- Can easily be biased (e.g. time of day, season, internet access etc.)
