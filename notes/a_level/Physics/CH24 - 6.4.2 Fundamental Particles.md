#Physics #Nuclear 

Antiparticles have the same mass and opposite charge as their corresponding particles 
The antiparticle of an electron ($e$) is the positron and is denoted $e^+$ 

There are 4 fundamental forces (in order of strength) : 
- Strong Nuclear - experienced between nucleons 
- Electromagnetic - experienced by charged particles 
- Weak Nuclear - responsible for beta-decay 
- Gravitational - experienced by mass 

**Hadrons** : 
- Sub-atomic particles / antiparticles e.g. protons, neutrons, mesons 
- Can experience all 4 fundamental forces 

**Leptons** : 
- Sub-atomic particles / antiparticles e.g. electrons, muons, neutrinos 
- Do not experience Strong Nuclear Force 

Quarks are fundamental particles 
Hadrons are made up of these fundamental particles 
e.g. a proton is $uud$ and a neutron is $udd$ 

| Name         | Symbol    | Charge Q / e   |
| ------------ | --------- | -------------- |
| up           | $u$       | $+\frac{2}{3}$ |
| down         | $d$       | $-\frac{1}{3}$ |
| strange      | $s$       | $-\frac{1}{3}$ |
| anti-up      | $\bar{u}$ | $-\frac{2}{3}$ |
| anti-down    | $\bar{d}$ | $+\frac{1}{3}$ |
| anti-strange | $\bar{s}$ | $+\frac{1}{3}$ |
Baryons are any hadron made using a combination of 3 quarks 
Mesons are hadrons made up of a quark and antiquark 
Final charge must be an integer 

**Neutrinos** : 
- Very small mass << electron 
- No charge 
- Is a lepton 
- There is an electron neutrino ($v_e$) and antineutrino ($\bar{v}_e$) 

Beta-minus ($\beta^-$) decay : $_0 ^1 n \rightarrow _1 ^1 p + _{-1} ^{\;\;0} e + \bar{v}_e$ 
Beta-plus ($\beta^+$) decay : $_1 ^1 p \rightarrow _0 ^1 n + _{+1} ^{\;\;0} e + v_e$
