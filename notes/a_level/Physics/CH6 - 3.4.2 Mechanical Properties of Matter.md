#Physics #Materials 

The work done on a spring is the area under a force-extension graph 
$W = \frac{1}{2} Fx = \frac{1}{2} kx^2$ 

$\text{Stress} = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}}$ or $\sigma = \frac{F}{A}$ (cross-sectional) 
$\text{Strain} = \frac{\text{Extension}}{\text{Length}}$ or $\mathcal{E} = \frac{x}{L}$ 
$\text{Young Modulus} = \frac{\text{Stress}}{\text{Strain}}$ or $E = \frac{\sigma}{\mathcal{E}}$ 
Higher Young Modulus means stiffer material 

When a material deforms elastically, it can return to its original shape 
When a material deforms plastically, it cannot return to its original shape

Definitions : 
- Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS) - highest stress material can endure before breaking 
- Brittle - no plastic deformation, UTS is breaking point 
- Strong - high UTS 
- Stiff - high Young Modulus 
- Ductile - easy to make thin 
- Polymeric - long molecular chains
