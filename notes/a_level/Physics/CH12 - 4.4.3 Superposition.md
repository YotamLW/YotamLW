The principle of superposition of waves : 
When two similar types of waves meet at a point, resultant displacement at that point is equal to the sum of the displacements of the individual waves 

Interference is when 2 or more progressive waves overlap and produce a resultant wave 
Coherence is when two waves have a constant phase difference 
Phase difference is the difference in angle between displacements of particles on a wave 
Path difference is the difference in distance travelled by two waves from their source to a point 

Because $I\propto A^2$, interference impacts intensity of the wave 
Constructive interference is when two waves are in phase and peaks line up with peaks 
Constructive interference is also when path difference of two coherent waves is $n\lambda$ 
Destructive interference is when two waves are in antiphase and peaks line up with troughs 
Destructive interference is also when path difference of two coherent waves is $\frac{1}{2}\lambda+n\lambda$

Young double-slit experiment : 
- Use a monochromatic source of light (colour filter for specific wavelength) 
- Single slit to diffract light 
- Double slit to create interference pattern 
- Classical confirmation for wave-nature of light 
![[4.4.3b]] 

Finding wavelength using double slit : 
Wavelength $\lambda=\frac{ax}{D}$ where $a$ is separation between two slits and $x$ is distance between fringes 
This equation uses small angle approximations so $a<<D$ where $D$ is distance of slits from pattern 

Finding wavelength using diffraction grating : 
The equation $d\sin\theta=n\lambda$ is used where $n$ is the order of maxima, $d$ is the distance between slits of the diffraction grating and $\theta$ is the angle between a straight line and a line joining to the maxima
