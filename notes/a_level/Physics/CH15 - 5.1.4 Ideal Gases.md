Number of particles in a mole $N_A = 6.02 \times 10^{23} \; mol^{-1}$ 
The mass of 1 mole of a substance is its mass number in grams 

The model of kinetic theory of gases is used to do gas calculations 
Assumptions for an ideal gas : 
- Large number of molecules in fast, random motion 
- Particles occupy negligible volume compared to volume of the gas 
- All collisions are perfectly elastic 
- Time of collisions is negligible compared to time between collisions 
- Negligible forces between particles except during collisions 

Pressure is caused by particles colliding with container walls elastically 
The particles change direction so their momentum changes 
Using Newton's second law $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$ so a force acts on the container 

$pV = nRT$ where $n$ is number of moles and $T$ is temperature 
$p \propto \frac{1}{V}$ (Boyle's law) and $p \propto T$ 
To investigate Boyle's law, plot a straight line graph of pressure on $\frac{1}{V}$ 
To estimate absolute 0, plot a straight line graph of pressure on temperature in Celsius 

$pV = \frac{1}{3} N m \bar{c}^2$ where $N$ is number of particles and $N = n \times N_A$ 
$\bar{c}$ is the root mean square speed and $\bar{c}^2$ is the mean square speed 
The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution shows that some particles may have more or less energy than the average energy / temperature which gives rise to unexpected occurrences

The internal energy of an ideal gas is the sum of the kinetic and potential energies of its particles
