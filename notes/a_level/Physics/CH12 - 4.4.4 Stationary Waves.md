#Physics #Waves 

Stationary / standing waves can be made using microwaves, stretched strings or air columns 
A standing wave has nodes (no particle movement) and antinodes (particles move to amplitude) 
![[4.4.4a]] 

Stationary waves are made by two waves of the same frequency travelling in opposite directions 
Nodes are where the progressive waves are in antiphase and antinodes where they are in phase 
Separation between adjacent nodes is $\frac{1}{2}\lambda$ where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the progressive wave 

| Progressive waves                                          | Stationary waves                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Energy transferred in direction of wave                    | No net energy transfer                                                                                 |
| Wavelength is minimum distance between two points in phase | Wavelength is twice the distance between adjacent nodes or antinodes                                   |
| Phase changes across one cycle of the wave                 | All parts of the wave between 2 nodes are in phase, and on either side of a node they are in antiphase |
| All parts of the wave have the same amplitude              | Maximum amplitude at antinodes and 0 at nodes                                                          |
| Has peaks and troughs                                      | Has peaks and troughs                                                                                  |
Microwave standing waves are made by reflecting a microwave with a metal sheet 

Harmonics are all possible standing waves that can form on a stretched string or air column 
The fundamental mode of vibration (1st harmonic) is the one with the lowest frequency 
The lowest frequency is called the fundamental frequency denoted as $f_0$ 
All other harmonics are multiples of $f_0$ 
Speed $v$ is constant so $\lambda\propto\frac{1}{f}$

Investigation to determine speed of sound : 
- Hold a vibrating tuning fork of known frequency $f_0$ above a closed resonance tube 
- Fill resonance tube gradually with water and keep testing until noise becomes loud 
- Measure length $L$ from open end to water level 
- This measurement $L=\frac{1}{4}\lambda$ 
- So speed of sound $v=f\lambda=f_0\times4L$
