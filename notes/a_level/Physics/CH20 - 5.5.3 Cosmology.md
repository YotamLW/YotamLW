Astronomical Unit (AU) : $1.5 \times 10^{11} m$ (average distance from Earth to the Sun) 
Light-year (ly) : $9.5 \times 10^{15} m$ (distance light travels in vacuum over a year) 
60 arcminutes is $1 \degree$ so 1 arcsecond is $(\frac{1}{3600}) \degree$ 
Parsec (pc) : $3.1 \times 10^{16} m$ (distance where 1AU subtends an arcsecond; $1pc = \frac{1AU}{tan(\frac{1}{3600})}$ 
Very small angle so small angle approximations appropriate 

Stellar Parallax is a method of determining the distance of nearby stars using distant stars 
![[5.5.3]] 
Can use $d = \frac{1}{p}$ up to 100pc where d is distance in parsecs and p is parallax angle in arcseconds 

The cosmological principle is the assumption that on a large enough scale the universe is homogenous, isotropic and the laws of physics are universal : 
- Homogenous means matter is distributed uniformly (the density is uniform) 
- Isotropic means the universe looks the same in all directions (no centre or edge) 
- Laws of physics are universal means it is assumed things behave the same everywhere 

The doppler effect is when the wavelength / frequency of waves changes for the observer because the emitting source is moving relative to the observer 
Doppler shift can happen to electromagnetic radiation 
Red-shifted means observed wavelength is longer (source moving away) 
Blue-shifted means observed wavelength is shorter (source moving towards) 
Doppler equation : $\frac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda} \approx \frac{\Delta f}{f} \approx \frac{v}{c}$ where $c$ is the speed of light 

Hubble's law : $v \approx H_0 d$ for receding galaxies ($H_0$ is a constant) 
$H_0 = 67.8\;km\;s^{-1}\;Mpc^{-1}$ or $H_0 = 2.2 \times 10^{-18}\;s^{-1}$ 

The model of an expanding universe suggests that space itself is expanding, hence why most stars appear redshifted from our perspective 
The big bang theory suggests all matter was in a singularity at the beginning of space and time 
Hence the big bang may have given rise to the expansion of space-time 
The main evidence is Hubble's law and microwave background radiation 

Microwave background radiation : 
- The big bang theory suggested that there used to be lots of gamma rays 
- Space has stretched over time so the wavelength of these gamma rays has increased 
- Treating the universe as a black body, its temperature is ~2.7 K 
- Using Wein's displacement law, the peak wavelength is microwave 

The age of the universe can be estimated by $t \approx H_0^{-1}$ 
The current idea of the universe is that it is made up of dark energy, dark matter and a small percentage of ordinary matter
