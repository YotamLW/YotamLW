#Physics #Astophysics

 Definition of a planet : 
- Orbits a star 
- Large enough mass to be round 
- Cleared its surroundings of other objects (e.g. asteroids) 
- No fusion 

Dwarf planets are not massive enough to clear their surroundings (e.g. Pluto) 
Asteroids are not massive enough to be round 
Comets are like asteroids but made of ice & rock and have more extreme orbits 
Planetary satellites orbit planets (e.g. moons) 

A solar system is a star and its orbiting bodies 
A galaxy is a collection of stars / solar systems 
The universe is everything 

Formation of a star : 
- Initially a cloud of interstellar gas (e.g. a nebula) 
- Gas cloud collapses due to gravity into a protostar (very hot and dense sphere of gas) 
- If massive enough, fusion of hydrogen into helium begins 
- This is now a star and its main sequence begins 
- Gas and radiation force push against gravity to keep the star stable :

Stars between 0.5$M_\odot$ and 10$M_\odot$ will become red giants
Stars with mass > 10$M_\odot$ will become red supergiants

Red giant : 
- Inert Core (not enough hydrogen and lower temperature) 
- Hydrogen fuses in shell around core 
- Star expands massively 
- Most of the star layers drift off forming a planetary nebula leaving a white dwarf 

Red supergiant : 
- Inert Iron core 
- Many layers of fusion (Silicon, Magnesium, Neon, Oxygen, Carbon, Helium, Hydrogen) 
- Iron nuclei do not fuse as Input $E$ > Output $E$ 
- Star collapses, bounces off core and creates supernova 
- Supernovae can outshine a galaxy and create all elements heavier than Iron 

After the Chandrasekhar limit (1.44$M_\odot$) electrons do not stop the star from collapsing 
If mass of core < 1.44$M_\odot$ the star becomes a white dwarf 
If mass of core > 1.44$M_\odot$ the star becomes neutron star 
If mass of core > 3$M_\odot$ the star becomes a black hole 

White dwarf : 
- Core of the leftover star 
- Mass $\approx M_\odot$ 
- Volume $\approx V_\oplus$ (Volume of Earth) 
- Leaks photons 
- Electron degeneracy pressure acts against gravity 

Neutron star : 
- Composed of neutrons 
- Density similar to atomic nucleus 
- Mass $\approx 2M_\odot$ 
- Diameter $\approx$ 10km 
- Neutron degeneracy pressure acts against gravity 
A pulsar is a rapidly spinning neutron star that emits radio waves 

Black hole : 
- Overcomes neutron degeneracy pressure 
- Gravitational field so strong escape velocity > speed of light at event horizon 
- Centre point of most galaxies (e.g. Milky way) 

**Hertzsprung-Russel diagram** : 
- Diagram of Luminosity (y) against Temperature (-x) 
- Shows relationship between Luminosity & Temperature with a star's lifecycle phase
