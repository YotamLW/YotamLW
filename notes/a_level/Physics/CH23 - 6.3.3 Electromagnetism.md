#Physics #Magnetism 

Magnetic flux $(\phi)$ is measured in weber ($Wb$) $\phi = BAcos\theta$ 
$\theta$ is the angle between the magnetic field and the normal to the cross-section 
Magnetic flux is the product of area with perpendicular magnetic flux density 

Magnetic flux linkage = $N\phi$ (measured in weber) where $N$ is the number of turns in a coil 
An e.m.f. is induced when there is a change in magnetic flux 

Faraday's law is that the magnitude of induced e.m.f. is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage or $\mathcal{E} \propto \frac{\Delta(N \phi)}{\Delta t}$ or $\mathcal{E} = - \frac{\Delta(N \phi)}{\Delta t}$  
Lenz's law is that the direction of induced e.m.f. / current is to oppose the change producing it 

A search coil can be used to investigate magnetic flux 
They are small coils of wire inserted into a magnetic field 

A.C. generators use metal moving in a magnetic field to produce an e.m.f. 
The metal is rotating in the field so its angle is constantly changing hence the alternating current 

For an ideal transformer $\frac{N_s}{N_p} = \frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{I_s}{I_p}$ 
$N$ is number of coils, $V$ is p.d. and $I$ is current 
Experiments with transformers will use simple laminated iron-cored transformers 
To investigate transformers, wrap coils around two iron cores and measure p.d. and current
