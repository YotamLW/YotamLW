#Physics #Waves 

Progressive waves transfer energy but not matter (matter oscillates) 
Transverse and Longitudinal waves are both progressive waves 

Transverse waves : 
- Oscillations are perpendicular to direction of energy transfer 
- Particles move up and down 
- Peak at highest point and trough at lowest 
- e.g. EM waves, water ripples, S-waves

![[4.4.1c]] 

Longitudinal waves : 
- Oscillations are parallel to direction of energy transfer 
- Particles move back and forth 
- Compressions at densest points and rarefactions at sparce points 
- e.g. sound waves, P-waves 
![[4.4.1b]] 

Definitions : 
- Displacement - distance of a particle from its equilibrium position 
- Amplitude - maximum displacement from equilibrium position (A) 
- Wavelength - minimum distance between two points in phase (e.g. peak to peak) ($\lambda$) 
- Period - time taken for one oscillation or for a wave to move one wavelength ($T$) 
- Frequency - wavelengths passing a point per unit time $f = \frac{1}{T}$ 
- Wave speed - Speed of a wave $v = f \lambda$ (wave equation) 
- Phase difference - The difference in angle between two points on a wave (or two different waves) where one wavelength is $2 \pi$ radians 

An oscilloscope will draw a graph of p.d. on time 
Using a recording device a wave can be turned into a signal and its frequency can be determined 

Reflection is when a wave changes direction at a medium boundary, remaining in its medium 
Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection (measured from the normal to the boundary) 
Refraction is when a wave changes direction when crossing a boundary between mediums due to a change in its speed 
![[4.4.1d]] 

Diffraction is when waves spread out after going through a gap 
Spread is affected by how close the width of the gap is to the wavelength 
A closer value means a better spread 
![[4.4.1e]] 

Polarisation is when waves are restricted to a certain plane of oscillation (plane polarised) 
Only applicable to transverse waves as longitudinal oscillate on a line 

Intensity = $\frac{\text{Power}}{\text{Area}}$ or $I = \frac{P}{A}$ 
Surface area of a sphere is $4\pi r^2$ so for a distance $r$ from a point light, Intensity $I = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2}$ 
Intensity $\propto$ (amplitude)$^2$
