Impulse = Force $\times$ time over which it acts 
Momentum $p=mv$ and is a vector 
Impulse = change in momentum or $F\Delta t=\Delta p$ 

A closed system will have constant total linear momentum in any direction 
An external impulse acting over a very small period of time can be regarded as negligible 

Assumptions when modelling direct impact : 
- If non-fixed objects in collisions are modelled as particles, all motion due to collisions acts along the same straight line 
- If non-fixed objects in collisions are modelled as bodies, these bodies will be uniformly circular 
- Collision and motion of these bodies will act along the line joining their centres 
- Collisions between objects and a plane occur perpendicularly 
- Objects will not rotate before or after collisions 

The coefficient of restitution is $e$ 
Newton's Experimental law : speed of separation = $e$ $\times$ speed of approach 
![[restitution]] 

When $e=1$ the collisions is perfectly elastic (kinetic energy is conserved) 
When $e<1$ kinetic energy is not conserved so find loss 
When $e=0$ the collision is inelastic (objects coalesce (merge))
