#Computer-Science 

Searching algorithms are only appropriate for sorted data 

Big O notation is used to describe the time & space complexities of algorithms 
O(1) constant means complexity is not dependant on number of items being handled 
O(n) linear means complexity scales linearly with number of items 
O(n$^2$) polynomial means complexity scaling polynomial with number of items 
O(2$^n$) exponential means complexity scaling is exponential with number of items 
O(logn) logarithmic means complexity scaling is logarithmic with number of items

## Bubble Sort 

Bubble sort is an in-place algorithm 
Repeated passes over list 
At each pass, every 2 neighbouring items are compared and swapped 
Ends when no swaps are made in a pass 
e.g. sorting the list \[1, 5, 3, 9, 2, 7] : 

| Unsorted | 1   | 5   | 3   | 9   | 2   | 7   |
| -------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pass 1   | 1   | 3   | 5   | 2   | 7   | 9   |
| Pass 2   | 1   | 3   | 2   | 5   | 7   | 9   |
| Pass 3   | 1   | 2   | 3   | 5   | 7   | 9   |
| Pass 4   | 1   | 2   | 3   | 5   | 7   | 9   |

## Insertion Sort 

Insertion sort is an in-place algorithm 
Slowly builds up the sorted list inserting each new item in the correct position 

## Merge Sort 

List of elements is repeatedly split into sub lists consisting of one element 
Two sub lists are compared at once 
First elements of both sub lists are compared and added to a new sub list in order 
If one of the two compared lists are empty then push the rest of the other list 
This produces a larger sorted sub list once the sub lists merge 
Repeated with larger sub lists until all sorted sub lists are recombined 

## Quick Sort 

Quicksort is a divide and conquer algorithm 
Quick sort decomposes data sets into subsets then sorts each subset then recombines 

For each data set, a pivot is chosen and start & end pointers are identified 
Items less than pivot are made into a subset and items larger are made into a separate subset 
Perform quicksort on each new subset made 
Recombine sub lists
