Graph Visualiser

Soon$^\text{TM}$ I will add a graph visualiser that will provide some useful information on each page and be a method of navigating between related pages. The basics it will show are its dependencies (pages that contain required prerequisite knowledge) and its dependents (pages that rely on the current page). I am debating on adding an "optional dependency" class. Within each page, I plan to make 3 versions for different uses. The primary page will be long-form and more text heavy, intended to teach the topic in detail and make it fully understandable. The second version will be shortened, intended as a note-like summary of the topic for use as a topic reminder or for exams. The third version will be any notes I may have on the topic, including where / why it is relevant / useful, thoughts I have on it etc. So, once I have enough pages, this will hopefully be a useful way to navigate and connect the topics together for learners.

My Thoughts

The point of WebIE (Website for Independent Education) is to produce a curriculum with a seamless connection between maths and science. Throughout Secondary and A-level, the maths in science is often simple (compared to maths lessons) and assumed. I think it would be much better for learning to teach these together from the start rather than waiting until University to start connecting topics. The main goals of this project is to come up with simpler ways of teaching these topics so people can understand them better in less time. Another goal is to make it enjoyable.

I think defining sciences as distinct subject areas is harmful, because it can limit consideration of things classified as "outside the topic". At University, there is more emphasis on starting to connect topics together, but this seems to be undoing the past classifications which could have been avoided in the first place.
