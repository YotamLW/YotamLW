Solids and fluids can be differentiated by their reaction to applied stresses. A solid resists shear stress by static deformation whereas a fluid deforms continuously for as long as it is applied. The continuum hypothesis treats fluids as a continuum rather than many finitely small particles with gaps, however molecular theory (finitely small particles) is used to calculate fluid properties associated with molecular motion (e.g. viscosity). Continuum hypothesis is not an appropriate model for rarefied gas dynamics (low pressures and density so molecules are far apart).
